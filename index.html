The problem with expense trackers

Most of them make you do the bookkeeping.

Type in every purchase, pick a category, remember to open the app. Holy Spend flips that. Forward a bill and it's logged, full stop. Itemize a grocery receipt instead and it goes further: per-unit pricing, price alerts, your own inflation number, a nutrition grade.

[ email ]

Forward a receipt email and it's already tracked

Amazon, Uber Eats, Instacart, your gym, your internet bill, whatever. It reads the email, checks it against what you've bought before, and it's in there. You never touch an app.

source: email · confirmed automatically
From here: itemized groceries
[ photo ]

A photo gets you every item, priced per unit

"$47.82 at Walmart" tells you nothing. This gets you 6 bananas at $1.52/kg, milk at $5.49 for 4L, every line separately, in a form you can actually compare the next time you buy the same thing.

12 lines · priced per kg / L / unit
[ price watch ]

It notices price changes you'd never catch yourself

It tracks price per unit on everything you buy more than once, sale sticker or not, and flags it the second something moves. Okra at FreshCo just doubled. Broccoli at Walmart is up 93%. Neither looked like a big deal at checkout.

+100.2% · okra, FreshCo
[ my inflation ]

One inflation number that's actually about you

Not the national number. Your own grocery basket, built only from things you buy more than once, priced honestly against what they cost you before.

+10.2% · 17 exact products
[ nutrition ]

A grocery grade that means something

A to E, weighted by what you actually spend on each thing, so a cart full of frozen pizza doesn't score the same as a cart full of vegetables just because they cost the same.

grade B · 94% coverage
